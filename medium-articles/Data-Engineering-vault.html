Real ETL pipelines, real data quality nightmares, and real domain knowledge across retail, healthcare, finance, and food service.

Each challenge lives in a different industry. Brazilian retail analytics. Healthcare data pipelines. Credit risk scoring. Food service operations. ETL pipeline craftsmanship. Five different domains, five different sets of constraints, and five different lessons that no textbook ever taught me.

The Core Concepts, Explained Simply

ETL (Extract, Transform, Load). Imagine you are moving apartments. First, you extract everything from the old place. Then you transform: sort through it, throw away what is broken, organize what is left. Finally, you load it into the new apartment in the right rooms. Every challenge in this vault follows that pattern. The art is in the Transform step, because that is where domain knowledge lives.

Key Insight: The Transform step is where junior engineers stumble most. It is not just cleaning nulls. It is understanding why a negative quantity makes no sense in a retail context, or why a date from 1900 in a healthcare system is actually a placeholder, not a real date.

Data Modeling. Data modeling is like designing a floor plan before building a house. If you put the bathroom where the kitchen should be, everything downstream breaks. Should you use a flat table or a star schema? The answer depends on who queries it, how fast they need results, and whether storage cost matters. That decision-making process is exactly what interviewers want to see.

Data Quality. The pipeline that moves data is only as good as the data it moves. I build quality gates at every stage: schema validation on ingest, null and range checks during transformation, row-count reconciliation after load. In the Boticario challenge, one missed formatting convention would have corrupted every calculation silently.

Idempotency. An idempotent pipeline produces the same result whether you run it once or ten times. Pipelines fail. Servers crash. When you retry, you do not want duplicate rows. I design for this using upserts, date-partitioned loads, and self-contained runs. Think of it like a light switch: flipping it once or five times still results in the light being on.

1. Boticario BR Challenge: Brazilian retail analytics. Handle locale-specific number formatting, seasonal sales patterns, and promotional cycles. The key lesson: never assume your data uses the same formatting conventions you are used to.

2. Home Credit Risk Engine: Financial credit risk modeling. Design a schema that captures applicant demographics, loan terms, and payment history. The key lesson: data modeling decisions have downstream consequences that compound.

3. Privia Health Challenge: Healthcare data pipeline with privacy constraints. Handle irregular timestamps, medical codes (ICD-10), and HIPAA considerations. The key lesson: domain-specific rules are not optional, they are the pipeline.

4. Prospa ETL Challenge: Pure pipeline craftsmanship. Extract from multiple sources, transform with business logic, load into a clean warehouse. The key lesson: idempotency is not academic. It saves you at 2 AM when the pipeline fails halfway through.

5. Gordon Food Service Drill: Speed round. Solve a data problem under pressure, exactly the way you would in an interview. The key lesson: you can not bluff your way through a live coding challenge. You either know SQL window functions or you do not.

What I Learned

  1. Domain knowledge is half the battle. You do not need to be a doctor to work with healthcare data, but you need to ask the right questions fast.
  2. SQL is a first-class citizen. Window functions, CTEs, self-joins, and pivoting are how you answer real business questions without spinning up a Spark cluster.
  3. Quality gates are non-negotiable. Schema validation on ingest, null checks during transform, row-count reconciliation after load.
  4. Idempotency saves you at 2 AM. Design every pipeline so that retrying it does not create duplicates.
  5. Documentation is part of the deliverable. Every decision I documented in these notebooks made the solution more valuable.

Why This Matters for Your Career

"Walk me through an ETL pipeline you built." Pick any challenge from this vault. Describe the source data, the quality issues you found, the transformation logic, and the output schema. Mention specific bugs you caught, like the Brazilian formatting issue.

"How do you ensure data quality?" Describe your three-layer approach: schema validation on ingest, business rule checks during transform, reconciliation counts after load. Give a concrete example from the Privia Health challenge.

"Star schema or flat table?" Explain the tradeoffs. Star schema gives you query flexibility and normalization. Flat tables are simpler and faster for single-use analytics. Reference the Home Credit project where this decision directly impacted query performance.

"How do you handle pipeline failures?" Talk about idempotency, checkpoint files, partitioned loads, and upserts. Explain how the Prospa challenge taught you to design for failure recovery.

"Tell me about a time you worked with unfamiliar domain data." Any of these challenges qualifies. The healthcare data had ICD-10 codes you had never seen. The retail data had formatting you did not expect. Walk through how you quickly learned enough domain context to build a correct pipeline.
